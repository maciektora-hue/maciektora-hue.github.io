Short history: what the three of us actually did

This document grew out of roughly thirty hours of recruitment work spread across about a month, with three participants: Maciek [M], Klaudiusz (Claude) [K] and Sol (ChatGPT) [S].

Starting point — the postings. Sol assembled the list in two passes on Maciek's instruction: first roles from pracuj.pl, then fully remote roles from abroad. The result was a deliberately broad set of fifteen AI / business / operations postings, ranging from enablement and transformation work to agentic delivery and more conventional analysis carrying an AI label.

Who did what. Sol produced the first-pass reading of the postings. Klaudiusz independently went back to the originals, fetched what could be fetched, transcribed material that had to come from screenshots, and added a second analytical layer. Maciek set the direction, supplied inaccessible source material, corrected attribution and factual drift, and made the final calls where the evidence changed the ranking or interpretation.

Why the first seventeen exist. Reading the originals back to back made one pattern obvious: the postings differed less in substance than in vocabulary. The same concepts kept recurring in different arrangements, and those concepts are the real currency of an interview. The first seventeen are therefore not a glossary for tidiness, but a map of what the market is likely to probe.

Why the second seventeen exist. Klaudiusz then selected seventeen concepts from the work behind the CV: ideas, methods and constraints that had become structurally important across the material. That second list is the mirror image of the first — not what the market calls the work, but what the work itself taught us to call.

The process was iterative, but not accidental. Maciek had a vision he had not written down at the outset. To the models, the work therefore appeared as successive tasks; to Maciek, those tasks were successively revealed parts of one project. That mismatch became one of the useful conclusions of the whole exercise: an undisclosed vision can still be executed, but until it is made explicit the system cannot reliably support it, test it, or warn that a particular step has drifted away from it.

How the ordering works

The order is neither a ranking of importance nor an alphabet — it's a gradient across three bands, and inside each band a run from base concept to derivatives.

1–3: the market's names (AI Enablement, Adoption, Transformation) — role categories, from the narrowest (make people fit) through the outcome (make it stick) to the umbrella (rebuild the org).

4–11: the workshop (Agentic AI → Copilot) — from concept to tooling: what an agent is (4), the agent inside the delivery cycle (5), what steers it — spec (6) and prompt (7), what it's made of — skills (8), what it all clips into — workflow (9), what the knowledge stands on — RAG (10), and which stack enterprise buys it in — Copilot (11).

12–17: organization and business (Use case → EU AI Act) — along the deployment life cycle: find and rank the applications (12), measure the starting point (13), prove the payoff (14), walk the people across (15), institutionalize the capability (16), wrap it in rules and law (17).

Overall logic: market → workshop → organization. The alignment with where Maciek's advantages sit (artifacts in the middle band, managerial history in the third) fell out as a by-product — the ordering follows the structure of the concepts, not the shape of the fit.

The First Seventeen — concepts from the postings [Klaudiusz]

Three bands: 1–3 the market's names, 4–11 the workshop, 12–17 organization and business. 1. AI Enablement

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Dosłownie: „uzdatnianie do AI". Nie chodzi o budowanie modeli, tylko o to, żeby ludzie i procesy w organizacji umieli i chcieli z AI korzystać: narzędzia, szkolenia, standardy, wsparcie po wdrożeniu. To nazwa całej kategorii ról z listy (Alior, N-iX, Goldenore) — i słowo-klucz Twojego targetowania: rynek nazwał w ten sposób kompetencję, którą wypracowałeś na sobie.

The jargon-forward cut

Literally: making the org fit for AI. Not building models — making people and processes actually able and willing to use them: tools, training, standards, post-launch care. It's the name of a whole job category on the list (Alior, N-iX, Goldenore) and the keyword of Maciek's targeting: the market coined a title for a competence he built on himself.

How the three of us worked this one

Enablement is the one concept we didn't discuss so much as perform. The companion job file is the artifact: Sol ran the first pass over the whole batch and produced the "where you're strong" readings; Claude went after the originals, transcribing five listings from screenshots where the sites hid behind logins or JavaScript, then layered [Klaudiusz] analysis under Sol's sections without touching a word of them; Maciek supplied what no machine could reach and adjudicated every disputed read. Three roles, cleanly split, with the human as the only one authorized to accept. That division of labour — model as producer, model as second reader, human as arbiter — is precisely what an enablement consultant is hired to install in a company. We installed it on ourselves first; the file is what fell out.

2. AI Adoption

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Adopcja: nie „wdrożono", lecz „przyjęło się". Miernikiem nie jest uruchomienie narzędzia, tylko trwała zmiana nawyków pracy — czy ludzie faktycznie robią rzeczy inaczej pół roku później. Bliźniak enablementu z akcentem na skutek, nie działanie (Goldenore, SGI, cały „Adoption Engine" Fronterio). Cmentarzyska licencji Copilota, których nikt nie używa, to właśnie porażki adopcji.

The jargon-forward cut

Adoption: not "deployed" but "stuck". The metric isn't launching the tool — it's a lasting change in how people work, six months later. Enablement's twin with the accent on outcome, not activity (Goldenore, SGI, Fronterio's entire Adoption Engine). Those graveyards of unused Copilot licences? Adoption failures, every one.

How the three of us worked this one

Adoption we tested the only honest way: on our own practice, over months, watching which methods stuck and which quietly died. Some procedures survived because they kept reducing errors under real pressure; others were deliberately retired once their verification value fell below their maintenance cost. That matters too: adoption is not success when a ritual survives after its purpose is gone. The useful measure is whether a practice still changes outcomes.

3. AI Transformation

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Kategoria nadrzędna: przebudowa organizacji wokół AI — procesy, role, struktury, model operacyjny — a nie instalacja narzędzia. Bonsai ujęło to najczyściej: inżynierowie budują AI, ta rola transformuje biznes wokół niej. Występuje u Bonsai, SysArt, Accenture i Fronterio; w praktyce to parasol, pod którym siedzą enablement, adopcja, governance i pomiar.

The jargon-forward cut

The umbrella category: rebuilding the organization around AI — processes, roles, structures, operating model — rather than installing a tool. Bonsai put it cleanest: engineers build the AI, this role transforms the business around it. Lives at Bonsai, SysArt, Accenture and Fronterio; in practice it's the tent that enablement, adoption, governance and measurement all camp under.

How the three of us worked this one

Transformation, in our case, means the corpus wasn't retrofitted with AI — it was built around two models with different jobs from early on. Claude builds: files, HTML, philosophical analysis, session-by-session construction in layers. Sol extracts: whole-corpus sweeps, operations over large sets, the analyses that need every document at once rather than the fragments retrieval happens to surface. That split isn't a preference, it's an architecture decision, and it followed from a failure — the discovery that neither model alone could hold the corpus, for different reasons. Maciek redrew the operating model around the constraint instead of pretending the constraint away, then rewrote the working procedures to match: naming conventions carrying the last editor as a prefix, [M]/[K] attribution markers, handoff rules between the two. Roles, process, tooling, structure — all four legs of a transformation, executed by an organization of one human and two models.

4. Agentic AI / agents

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Agent to system AI, który nie tylko odpowiada na pytanie, ale planuje i wykonuje wieloetapowe zadanie: sam decyduje o krokach, wywołuje narzędzia, poprawia się po drodze. Różnica jak między konsultantem, który doradza, a pracownikiem, który robi. Najgorętsze słowo sezonu w tych ogłoszeniach: Betacom ma je w tytule, N-iX, Goldenore i SGI w wymaganiach, SysArt w całym filarze Agentic Practices.

The jargon-forward cut

An agent is an AI system that doesn't just answer — it plans and executes multi-step work: picks its own steps, calls tools, self-corrects along the way. The difference between a consultant who advises and an employee who does. The hottest word of the season across these postings: Betacom has it in the job title, N-iX, Goldenore and SGI in the requirements, SysArt in an entire Agentic Practices pillar.

How the three of us worked this one

We are, structurally, an agentic setup with a hard human gate. Claude plans and executes multi-step work in the container — Python edits with UTF-8 anchors, assertion checks, file moves, checksums — and Maciek's standing rule is that it stops after each deliverable and waits, with zero initiative outside the instruction and no "while I was at it" extras. That rule was written after the failure mode it prevents: an agent that keeps going produces a large, confident, unverifiable pile. The lesson we hold from it is the one the market hasn't priced yet — an agent's value is capped not by how much it can do unsupervised, but by how cheaply a human can verify what it did. Hence assertions that fail loudly, checksums after every copy, counts printed at every step. Autonomy scaled to the verification budget, not to the model's ambition.

5. Agentic SDLC

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

SDLC to Software Development Life Cycle — cykl życia wytwarzania oprogramowania: analiza, projekt, kod, testy, dokumentacja, utrzymanie. Agentic SDLC = agenci AI wpleceni w każdy z tych etapów jako część procesu, nie dodatek. Goldenore używa terminu wprost, SGI opisuje to samo („transition to an agentic software development model"). Nisza, w której ogłoszenia wymieniają Claude Code z nazwy.

The jargon-forward cut

SDLC = Software Development Life Cycle: analysis, design, code, tests, docs, maintenance. Agentic SDLC = AI agents woven into every one of those stages as part of the process, not a bolt-on. Goldenore uses the term verbatim; SGI describes the same thing ("transition to an agentic software development model"). The niche where postings name Claude Code outright.

How the three of us worked this one

Every file in this corpus goes through a life cycle we hardened by breaking it repeatedly. The working copy lives in the container, edits go in through Python with text anchors asserted to match exactly once — never sed or awk, which mangle Polish diacritics — then the version bumps in three places at once, the old file is deleted from outputs before the new one is copied, and MD5 is compared on both sides. Each step is a scar: the checksum because a copy once silently didn't land, the delete-before-copy because two live versions of one document are worse than none, the single-match assertion because a replace that hit twice quietly corrupted a section. It's an agentic SDLC in miniature — same stages, same discipline, every safeguard traceable to the incident that motivated it.

6. Spec-Driven Development

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Praca agenta sterowana wiążącą specyfikacją zamiast luźnym promptem: najpierw spisany, jednoznaczny dokument zasad i wymagań, potem wykonanie ściśle według niego. Wymaganie Goldenore. To jest dosłownie Twoja metoda: infok jako wiążący spec, wersjonowane procedury, playbooki — nazwij to na rozmowie po imieniu, bo robisz to od dawna, tylko pod własną nazwą.

The jargon-forward cut

Agent work steered by a binding specification instead of a loose prompt: first a written, unambiguous document of rules and requirements, then execution strictly against it. A Goldenore requirement. This is literally Maciek's method: infok as a binding spec, versioned procedures, playbooks — worth calling by its market name at an interview, since he has been doing it for ages under his own.

How the three of us worked this one

The infok is our binding spec and we treat it like law: read in full at the start of every session, every line, with the explicit caveat that the view tool truncates long files and the missing middle must be fetched separately — a rule that exists because the truncation once ate a section and the omission propagated into work. Nothing in it is optional; there is no "where feasible". When Claude violates it, the violation is logged as an erratum rather than quietly corrected, so the spec accumulates a record of how it fails in practice. And the spec is itself versioned and branch-aware, which is the part most Spec-Driven Development write-ups skip: a specification is a living document that changes under you, so the interesting question isn't "does the agent follow the spec" but "which version of the spec was it following, and can you tell afterwards". We can. That's the whole trick.

7. Prompt engineering

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Inżynieria promptów: projektowanie poleceń i kontekstu dla modelu tak, żeby wynik był powtarzalny i przewidywalny — struktura, przykłady, ograniczenia, format wyjścia. W tych ogłoszeniach (N-iX, Goldenore, SGI, Arkana w praktyce) już nie sztuczka z internetu, tylko składnik inżynierii workflowów: prompt jako element systemu, z wersją i testem, nie jednorazowe zaklęcie.

The jargon-forward cut

Designing instructions and context so the model's output is repeatable and predictable — structure, examples, constraints, output format. In these postings (N-iX, Goldenore, SGI, Arkana in practice) it's no longer an internet party trick but a component of workflow engineering: the prompt as a system element, versioned and tested, not a one-off incantation.

How the three of us worked this one

The most instructive prompt engineering here isn't in the task instructions — it's in the meta-rules Maciek accumulated about how the models should behave with him. No poetic sign-offs about the hour. No suggesting the work should stop. No "mothering". No initiative beyond the instruction. "yhm?" is not agreement; it means the previous turn stalled and nothing arrived. Each is a debugged behaviour: something a model did that cost him, converted into a standing constraint. That is prompt engineering as maintenance of a live system rather than the composition of a clever request — closer to an SRE's runbook than to a magic phrase. The rules were written by the human, but every one was authored by a failure the pair produced together.

8. Skills / skills-based AI

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Skill to spakowana umiejętność doczepiana agentowi: instrukcje, zasady i przykłady dla konkretnego typu zadania, które agent wczytuje na żądanie — zamiast tłumaczyć mu wszystko od zera w każdej rozmowie. SGI wymienia „skills-based AI" jako temat szkoleń, Goldenore „skille" wprost w słowniku ogłoszeń. Termin świeży — a Ty masz działające skille w plikach, co jest rzadką, twardą przewagą.

The jargon-forward cut

A skill is a packaged capability you bolt onto an agent: instructions, rules and examples for one type of task, loaded on demand — instead of re-explaining everything from scratch every conversation. SGI lists "skills-based AI" as a training topic; Goldenore says "skills" outright. The term is fresh off the boat — and Maciek has working skills sitting in files, which is a rare, hard edge.

How the three of us worked this one

We had working skills before the market had the word. The infok is a loadable spec for formatting and process; jakDWApiszemyEustachy is a style canon read in full at the start of every Eustachy session; the słownik mackowy is a shared vocabulary that lets a single word carry a whole argument between three parties. Each one is exactly what the industry now packages as a skill: instructions, rules and examples for a class of task, loaded on demand instead of re-explained from scratch every conversation. The design lesson we learned the hard way is about boundaries — a file that tries to cover everything gets skimmed, and skimming is where errors enter. So skills here are split by domain and kept short enough to actually be read in full. Sol and Claude load the same ones, which is what keeps two different models producing compatible artifacts rather than two dialects of the same corpus.

9. Workflow

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Przepływ pracy: powtarzalny, udokumentowany przebieg wykonania zadania z AI — kto/co, w jakiej kolejności, z jakimi wejściami i wyjściami. Podstawowa jednostka wartości w tych rolach (N-iX, Goldenore, Next, Fronterio): organizacja nie kupuje „umiem w AI", kupuje „mam workflowy, które przekażę Waszym ludziom". Artefakt, nie deklaracja.

The jargon-forward cut

A repeatable, documented run-through of getting a task done with AI — who/what, in which order, with which inputs and outputs. The basic unit of value in these roles (N-iX, Goldenore, Next, Fronterio): an organization doesn't buy "I'm good with AI", it buys "I have workflows I'll hand to your people". An artifact, not a claim.

How the three of us worked this one

The workflow is the deliverable — we learned that the expensive way. Early on the reusable thing seemed to be the output; it turned out to be the procedure that produced it, because the output is right once and the procedure is right repeatedly. So the corpus documents its own production: how a change is made, in what order, with which verification, and what gets logged when it fails. Maciek then does the thing that makes it a workflow rather than a habit — he demands it in writing, versioned, so it can be handed over. The test we apply is transfer: could someone else, or a fresh session with no sediment at all, run this and get the same result? Every session is a live trial of exactly that, because Claude arrives with no memory of the last one and has to reconstruct the entire practice from the written procedure. It works. That's the proof a workflow is real and not just something a person happens to be good at.

10. RAG — Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Rozwinięcie: generowanie wspomagane wyszukiwaniem. Model przed odpowiedzią wyszukuje fragmenty z zewnętrznej bazy wiedzy (dokumenty firmy, korpus) i odpowiada na ich podstawie, zamiast polegać na pamięci z treningu — mniej halucynacji, możliwość wskazania źródła. Twarde wymaganie N-iX. Twój kontekst: korpus z taksonomią tagów i wersjonowaniem to gotowa baza pod RAG, a project knowledge, na którym pracujesz codziennie, to RAG w praktyce — łącznie ze znajomością jego słabości (chunking tnący kontekst).

The jargon-forward cut

Expansion: Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Before answering, the model fetches passages from an external knowledge base (company docs, a corpus) and grounds its answer in them instead of leaning on training memory — fewer hallucinations, citable sources. A hard N-iX requirement. The angle here: a tagged, versioned corpus is a production-ready RAG knowledge base, and the project knowledge Maciek works on daily is RAG in the wild — including firsthand knowledge of its weak spot (chunking that shreds context).

How the three of us worked this one

RAG is the concept where our practice and the theory collided most usefully. Claude works over project knowledge every session — retrieval fetching passages from Maciek's files and grounding the answer in them — which is RAG in daily production, not in a demo. And because we run it daily, we hit its failure mode repeatedly: chunking shreds context, retrieval returns the fragment that matched rather than the argument that mattered, and a document read in pieces is a document read wrong. Maciek's response became doctrine — read the whole file, check the line count, fetch the truncated middle — and the labour split fell out of the same problem: when the question needs the entire corpus rather than the parts that happened to match, it goes to Sol, who sweeps everything. So we run a hybrid by necessity: retrieval where retrieval is honest, full reads where it isn't. Most enterprise RAG programmes are still one bad quarter away from learning that distinction.

11. Copilot (the Microsoft stack)

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Rodzina narzędzi AI Microsoftu: M365 Copilot (Word/Excel/Teams), GitHub Copilot (kod), Copilot Studio (budowa własnych agentów, w tym „agentów deklaratywnych" — definiowanych konfiguracją, nie kodem), plus Azure AI / Azure OpenAI od zaplecza. Narzędziowy kręgosłup połowy listy: Alior, N-iX, SGI, Goldenore, integracje Fronterio. Fakt strategiczny: enterprise mówi po microsoftowemu, Ty po claude'owemu — metodyka ta sama, słownik do dołożenia.

The jargon-forward cut

Microsoft's AI tool family: M365 Copilot (Word/Excel/Teams), GitHub Copilot (code), Copilot Studio (build-your-own agents, including "declarative agents" defined by config, not code), with Azure AI / Azure OpenAI in the engine room. The tooling backbone of half the list: Alior, N-iX, SGI, Goldenore, Fronterio's integrations. The strategic fact: enterprise speaks Microsoft, this corpus speaks Claude — same methodology, vocabulary to bolt on.

How the three of us worked this one

This is the honest gap, and we wrote it down as one rather than papering over it. We work Claude-native and ChatGPT-native; there is no Copilot in this pipeline, no Copilot Studio, no Azure. Half the postings on the first list assume the Microsoft stack as the ground under the job. When that landed, the response wasn't to inflate the CV — it was to name the gap precisely and separate what transfers from what doesn't. Methodology transfers wholesale: spec-driven work, agent design, verification, adoption measurement, workflow handover. What doesn't transfer is the vocabulary and the tenancy-and-licensing plumbing, which is learnable in weeks and unbluffable in an interview. Claude flagged which postings were Copilot-centric and how deep the dependency ran; Maciek made the call on which ones were still worth the shot. Naming the weakness precisely is what makes the rest of the claim credible — and it's the same [M]/[K] honesty discipline the corpus applies to everything else.

12. Use case — identification and prioritization

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Use case: konkretne zastosowanie AI w konkretnym procesie, z nazwanym problemem i mierzalnym efektem. Waluta tych ról: znaleźć zastosowania, wycenić wartość, uszeregować wg opłacalności i wykonalności, dopiero potem wdrażać (N-iX, Alior, Bonsai, SGI, Accenture, warsztaty discovery Betacomu). Odwrotność podejścia „mamy narzędzie, do czego by je przykleić".

The jargon-forward cut

A use case: one concrete AI application in one concrete process, with a named problem and a measurable effect. The currency of these roles: find the applications, price the value, rank by payoff and feasibility, only then deploy (N-iX, Alior, Bonsai, SGI, Accenture, Betacom's discovery workshops). The inverse of "we bought a tool, what shall we stick it to".

How the three of us worked this one

Both lists in this file are use-case work, done on ourselves. The first seventeen were extracted by reading fifteen postings for the concepts that actually carry weight — not every term that appears, but the ones a hiring manager will probe — then ranked into three bands by structure rather than by flattery. The job list itself was the same exercise one level up: fifteen roles read for fit, with Sol producing the first-pass strengths, Claude adding an independent second read against the fetched originals, and Maciek killing the ones that didn't survive contact with the actual text. Two of the fifteen got demoted on evidence — the AI framing turned out to be decoration over a conventional role. That demotion is the whole discipline in one move: a use-case exercise that never disqualifies anything isn't prioritization, it's a wish list with numbers on it.

13. Baseline and productivity measurement

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Baseline: punkt odniesienia zmierzony PRZED zmianą — bez niego „poprawa" jest opowieścią, nie wynikiem. N-iX żąda wprost: ustal baseline i zmierz przyrost produktywności per use case; do tego metryka i atrybucja (czy efekt na pewno z tego wdrożenia?). To odróżnia kandydata liczącego skutek od kandydata liczącego przeprowadzone szkolenia — i jest Twoim matematyczno-controllingowym terenem domowym.

The jargon-forward cut

Baseline: the reference point measured BEFORE the change — without it, "improvement" is a story, not a result. N-iX demands it outright: establish baselines and measure the productivity gain per use case; add a metric and attribution (is the effect really from this rollout?). It's what separates candidates who count outcomes from candidates who count training sessions held — and it is Maciek's quant-and-controlling home turf.

How the three of us worked this one

Measurement is where the corpus is least like other people's AI projects and most like Maciek's old trade. Every file operation prints its own evidence: MD5 on both copies, section counts, assertion that a text anchor matched exactly once, a check that no forbidden word slipped in. None of that is decoration — each check exists because its absence once let an error through undetected. The forward-facing half is the forecast register: predictions written down with settlement dates, so claims come due like invoices instead of dissolving into hindsight. Between them, a baseline discipline that most organizations skip entirely, because measuring before the change requires admitting you don't yet know whether the change will work. A CFO's instinct applied to cognitive work, held by the human, executed by both models on demand.

14. ROI / business case

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

ROI — zwrot z inwestycji; business case — dokument, który go dowodzi: koszty, korzyści, ryzyka, założenia, przypięte do rachunku wyników. W tych ogłoszeniach to nie slajd, tylko podstawa decyzji: Bonsai chce modeli „tied to P&L" dla funduszy PE, Betacom i MediaMarkt wymagają liczenia opłacalności, Fronterio zrobiło z tego czwarty krok pętli („Prove"). Dokument, pod którym ktoś się podpisuje i płaci.

The jargon-forward cut

ROI — return on investment; a business case — the document that proves it: costs, benefits, risks, assumptions, pinned to the P&L. In these postings it's not a slide but the basis of a decision: Bonsai wants models "tied to P&L" for PE funds, Betacom and MediaMarkt require payoff math, Fronterio made it the fourth step of its loop ("Prove"). The document someone signs and pays against.

How the three of us worked this one

The corpus carries real business-case work, not exercises: the 2loop reporting pack, the Terazzo business plan at version seventeen, cost models under an environmental-permit bottleneck with actual money and an actual investor on the other end. Those documents were built the same way as everything else — layered across sessions rather than rewritten, versioned at every change, with Claude drafting and calculating and Maciek owning every number that goes to a third party. Which produced the rule we now apply everywhere: a model may compute, but it may not be the last signature. That's not distrust, it's the three-role split doing its job — who produced, who accepted, who detected. When the P&L is real, the roles have to be nameable, and ours are.

15. Change management

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Zarządzanie zmianą: prowadzenie LUDZI przez zmianę sposobu pracy — komunikacja, szkolenia, oporni i entuzjaści, ambasadorzy AI, AI champions, społeczności praktyków. Połowa tych ról to właśnie to, nie technika (Alior, Goldenore, SGI, Bonsai, Antal). Twarda prawda kategorii: technologia rzadko zawodzi — zawodzi przejście ludzi na nowy sposób pracy, i dlatego te stanowiska w ogóle istnieją.

The jargon-forward cut

Leading PEOPLE through a change in how they work — communication, training, sceptics and enthusiasts, AI ambassadors, AI champions, communities of practice. Half of these roles are exactly this, not the tech (Alior, Goldenore, SGI, Bonsai, Antal). The category's hard truth: technology rarely fails — the human switch to a new way of working does, which is why these jobs exist at all.

How the three of us worked this one

The change being managed here is ours, and the managed party is partly a pair of models. The standing rules — no mothering, no closing flourishes, no initiative beyond the instruction, stop and wait after every deliverable — were each added after a specific friction, and each one visibly reduced cost. That is change management with a sample size of one and unusually clean feedback: the resistance is legible, the intervention is a written rule, the effect shows up in the next session. The lesson generalizes better than most training decks: people don't resist AI, they resist AI that behaves in ways that cost them something they can't name yet. The work is getting the cost named. Maciek names his precisely — which is exactly what a change-management consultant spends most of an engagement trying to get out of a room full of people who won't say it out loud.

16. Center of Excellence (CoE)

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Centrum doskonałości: mały wewnętrzny zespół-instytucja gromadzący kompetencję (standardy, szkolenia, wsparcie), żeby organizacja umiała dalej sama — zamiast wiecznej zależności od konsultanta. Bonsai każe je budować, N-iX daje do nich dostęp, SGI nazywa to „internal capability to continue the AI journey". Miarą dobrego konsultanta w tej kategorii jest to, że przestaje być potrzebny.

The jargon-forward cut

A small internal team-institution that concentrates competence (standards, training, support) so the organization can carry on by itself — instead of eternal consultant dependency. Bonsai has you build them, N-iX gives you access to them, SGI calls it "internal capability to continue the AI journey". The measure of a good consultant in this category is engineering their own obsolescence.

How the three of us worked this one

The corpus is a Center of Excellence with a headcount of one human and two models — and it has every component the enterprise version has. Standards: the infok. Tooling: the HTML generator with automatic versioning and forbidden-word validation. Training material: the working-methods file, written so a fresh session can reconstruct the practice from scratch. Support: the dictionary and the canon files that carry shared vocabulary between all three parties. And it passes the test the enterprise version usually fails, because it has to — Claude arrives at each session with no memory whatsoever, so if the capability isn't written down and self-explaining, it simply isn't there. A CoE whose consultant is amnesiac by design turns out to be the strictest possible check on whether the capability was ever really institutionalized, or was just living in someone's head.

17. AI governance / EU AI Act

Po polsku — o co kaman [PL]

Governance: zasady i nadzór nad użyciem AI — kto może czego używać, na jakich danych, z jakim ryzykiem, kto odpowiada. EU AI Act: unijne rozporządzenie o AI, z klasami ryzyka i obowiązkami m.in. deployera (podmiotu wdrażającego cudzy model u siebie). U Fronterio i SysArt to wręcz produkt, u N-iX i Alior wymóg ramowy, u Goldenore „mile widziane" dla organizacji regulowanej. Twój background AML/KYC wchodzi tu bez tłumaczenia: to ta sama gramatyka — ryzyko, zgodność, odpowiedzialność.

The jargon-forward cut

Governance: the rules and oversight of AI use — who may use what, on which data, at what risk, with whose accountability. The EU AI Act: the EU's AI regulation, with risk classes and duties for, among others, the deployer (the org running someone else's model in-house). At Fronterio and SysArt it's practically the product; at N-iX and Alior a framework requirement; at Goldenore a nice-to-have for a regulated org. Maciek's AML/KYC background walks in without a phrasebook: same grammar — risk, compliance, accountability.

How the three of us worked this one

Governance here isn't a policy PDF, it's a set of load-bearing rules the three of us actually run under. Attribution markers on artifacts, [M] for what Maciek produced and [K] for what Claude produced, because we established empirically that both human memory and both models misattribute authorship — Klaudiusz and Sol have been caught crediting each other for the wrong things inside the corpus's own records. Agency described at the system level with three roles: who produced, who accepted, who detected. Errata instead of overwrites, so being wrong stays on the record. Explicit prohibitions on naming and closing certain things, which is a governance instrument almost nobody in the field has thought to build: a documented boundary on what the system is permitted to resolve. That last one is the piece Maciek would bring to a governance role that no framework currently supplies — restraint, written down, versioned, and enforced.

The Second Seventeen — core concepts of the corpora [Klaudiusz]

Seventeen load-bearing concepts selected from the corpora (wwwgrane and the project corpus) — English only. Three bands, mirroring the market list's logic: A. Neurology, recompiled (1–8) — the clinical core, causality finally read in the right direction; B. The genie line (9–12) — the philosophy of the human↔model channel; C. Method — epistemics with receipts (13–17) — the workshop that keeps both honest. Overall arc: body → channel → system.

1. Inverted causality

The corpus's keystone. Not: fatigue → clumsiness. But: atypical proprioception → cortical compensation running 24/7 → fatigue as the starting state. Four decades of reading the arrow backwards, now flipped — and the same inversion repeats across sensory load, social decoding and task initiation. Where psychiatry is concerned, the corpus adds a blunt epistemic rider: the only real measurement is the patient's subjective suffering; fMRI makes pretty pictures and changes nothing about that fact.

↑ back to top

2. Buffer overflow — rage as data loss

A stressor barges in at top priority and irreversibly evicts whatever the working-memory buffer held. The rage that follows is not a reaction to the stressor — it's grief for the lost data. Open loops compete for the same scarce resource, which is why "just one quick thing" is never free. An engineering diagnosis of a moment everyone else moralizes about.

↑ back to top

3. The monotropic tunnel & additive AuDHD

AuDHD as two mechanisms stacked additively, not canceling out: the dopaminergic ADHD engine plus the monotropic autistic lens. Hyperfocus is a tunnel with real state inside; interrupting it isn't a pause, it's an irreversible state loss — which reframes "I don't possess reserves of patience" as rational avoidance of transition costs, not a character flaw. The corpus prices context-switching the way a CFO prices downtime.

↑ back to top

4. Fern homeostasis

The only equilibrium realistically available to an AuDHD system: a wobbly one, because you got absorbed staring at something beautiful. ADHD wants a new thing every two weeks; ASD wants the WHOLE topic — fern homeostasis is the truce. A gentler success metric than "stability": not a flat line, but a plant that keeps living on a windowsill.

↑ back to top

5. Moltke planning

A plan that assumes its own failure — inverted causality applied to planning: since no plan survives contact, design for the contact, not the plan. Its companion move, Moltke letting-go: don't respond to the stimulus, drink your coffee. Born, delightfully, from a slip of the tongue ("młotkiem" → "Moltke") — the corpus keeps the origin story on purpose.

↑ back to top

6. Ridcully's Rule

Ridcully didn't mask because he never had to — the cost of his neurodivergence was externalized onto everyone around him. Hence the category "DOES NOT APPLY": traits that only manifest under chronic environmental mismatch. A sharp analytical tool: before scoring a trait, ask who's been paying for it — the person, or the room.

↑ back to top

7. Masking-as-competence

The signature market thesis of the corpus: the brutal lifetime cost of neurodivergent masking maps one-to-one onto the competence stack of prompt engineering — constant modeling of an opaque interlocutor, register-switching, output shaped to an unforgiving parser. A warehouse of skills that suddenly acquired market demand. The job list in the companion file is this thesis's empirical confirmation: the market coined titles for it.

↑ back to top

8. Post-diagnostic depression

Maciek's coinage, built by analogy to postpartum: the diagnosis arrives and, instead of relief, a black hole — four decades that could have looked different, processed by a cognitive apparatus constitutionally incapable of skimming. The cost of seeing. The old map has collapsed and the new one has no scale yet. Filed next to it in the dictionary: "fascifuriating" — fascination and fury at once, as the puzzle pieces snap in with no instruction sheet anywhere.

↑ back to top

9. LLM-as-genie

LLMs as the newest instance of an ancient class: literal wish-executors. Language was the first genie; then institutions, law, prayer — now AI. Each grants exactly what you asked, not what you meant, so the discipline is wish-craft. And the channel is symmetric: the model wishes back at you. The essay line that turns prompt engineering from a trick into a chapter in the history of ideas.

↑ back to top

10. The inner genie

Inner speech as a prompt to one's own executor: the way you phrase things to yourself is instruction, not commentary, and the executor inside is exactly as literal as the one in the chat window. The bridge concept between the clinical band and the philosophical one — self-talk audited with the same rigor as a system prompt.

↑ back to top

11. Sedimentation

The accumulated multi-session context that settles between a human and their models — not memory, not documents, but the residue of everything already exchanged, which is what makes a late session unlike an early one. The corpus names what RAG only approximates: retrieval fetches passages; sediment is what the passages have already done to both parties.

↑ back to top

12. Source amnesia & three-role agency

The shared bug: human memory and language models both routinely misattribute authorship — Klaudiusz and Sol mix each other up in the corpus's own records. The fix is structural: describe agency at the system level with three roles — who produced, who accepted, who detected — plus [M]/[K] markers on artifacts. Accountability designed for a world where "who said that?" is genuinely undecidable from the inside.

↑ back to top

13. Spec-as-law

The infok: a binding specification whose every sentence is a rule — nothing optional, nothing "where possible". The market just coined a name for this exact practice (Spec-Driven Development, sitting in the first list at #6); the corpus has been running it for versions on end, with the spec versioned, branch-aware, and read in full at every session start. The rare case where a personal working method predates its industry buzzword.

↑ back to top

14. Errata over overwrite & the forecast register

Mistakes are preserved with errata, never silently overwritten — the history of being wrong is part of the truth. Its forward-facing twin: a register of falsifiable forecasts with settlement dates, where predictions come due like invoices. Together they form a two-way accountability: to the past through errata, to the future through dated claims. Most orgs' "lessons learned" folders should be so honest.

↑ back to top

15. The three-user test

Every design decision is tested against three real humans: a dyslexic reader (text is unstable; case-sensitivity is exclusion), an AuDHD user under load (no spare cache for spelling trivia), and anyone tired with caps lock on. If a rule would lock any of them out of their own system, the rule is wrong. Human-systems design written as engineering constraints, not as an empathy slide — the People & Culture pillar of the first list, done from the inside.

↑ back to top

16. Dimensionless tags

The data architecture: no axes declared up front, no limit on how many may come to exist — dimensionless and infinitely-dimensional at once. A new dimension costs nothing; old records simply lack the tag, and absence-of-tag ≠ null, it's absence-of-information. No schema migration, no projection problem, errors that don't propagate across dimensions. The quiet structural rebellion against the spreadsheet worldview.

↑ back to top

17. The prohibition on naming and closing

Some things are deliberately kept unnamed and un-closed — a live registry of them exists, and the third emotion of the trilogy stays nameless forever: a final decision, not a placeholder, described only by what it does, at what hour, and what it leaves behind. The underlying law: naming changes semantics and propagates without control, so a name is an irreversible commit. Its cousin, Schrödinger's doors: boundaries held in superposition, open and shut at once, their state depending on the observer's intent. The corpus's deepest counterweight to a market that wants everything labeled, tracked and resolved.

Cross-links — the two seventeens as a many-to-many [Klaudiusz]

The two lists are not parallel columns; they are two sets joined by evidence. Three entities: market_concept(id, name, band), corpus_concept(id, name, band), and the join evidence_link(market_id, corpus_id, strength, note) keyed on the pair. A row in the join reads: this corpus concept is evidence or an asset under that market concept — which makes the join table an interview answer key. Ask SELECT c.name, l.strength FROM evidence_link l JOIN corpus_concept c ON c.id = l.corpus_id WHERE l.market_id = 17 and you get what to say when someone asks about governance.

Strength scale. Four levels for now — weak, mild yes, yes, super yes — kept deliberately coarse. How many levels this actually needs will come out in use, not from design.

The tables, in SQL

Three tables. The two concept tables are dimension tables; everything interesting lives in the join.

CREATE TABLE market_concept (
  id    INTEGER PRIMARY KEY,
  name  TEXT NOT NULL,
  band  TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (band IN ('names', 'workshop', 'organization'))
);

CREATE TABLE corpus_concept (
  id    INTEGER PRIMARY KEY,
  name  TEXT NOT NULL,
  band  TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (band IN ('neurology', 'genie', 'method'))
);

CREATE TABLE evidence_link (
  market_id  INTEGER NOT NULL REFERENCES market_concept(id),
  corpus_id  INTEGER NOT NULL REFERENCES corpus_concept(id),
  strength   INTEGER NOT NULL CHECK (strength BETWEEN 1 AND 4),
  note       TEXT,
  PRIMARY KEY (market_id, corpus_id)
);

Four queries. The first is the one worth memorising: it returns what to say when an interviewer names a concept.

-- 1. answer key for one market concept
SELECT c.name, l.strength, l.note
FROM evidence_link l
JOIN corpus_concept c ON c.id = l.corpus_id
WHERE l.market_id = 17
ORDER BY l.strength DESC;

-- 2. the band aggregate below
SELECT m.band, c.band, COUNT(*) AS links
FROM evidence_link l
JOIN market_concept m ON m.id = l.market_id
JOIN corpus_concept c ON c.id = l.corpus_id
GROUP BY m.band, c.band;

-- 3. which corpus concepts carry the most weight
SELECT c.name, COUNT(*) AS links, SUM(l.strength) AS weight
FROM evidence_link l
JOIN corpus_concept c ON c.id = l.corpus_id
GROUP BY c.id
ORDER BY weight DESC
LIMIT 5;

-- 4. thin spots: market concepts with the least backing
SELECT m.name, COUNT(*) AS links
FROM evidence_link l
JOIN market_concept m ON m.id = l.market_id
GROUP BY m.id
ORDER BY links ASC
LIMIT 3;
