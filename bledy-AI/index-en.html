Shelf of shame

A register of the mistakes made while working on this repository. Kept with numbers and quotes rather than apologies, because a register of mistakes without numbers slides into a literary genre in which the author comes off rather well. Three shelves since 2026-09-20: two models and the human who commissions the work. The third was added because a register covering only the models measures half the system — an instruction that says something other than what its author means yields a bad result however faultlessly it is carried out. The model counters were reset deliberately — see the note below.

The numbers on both sides measure volume of work, not quality of model. SOL built this repository — set up the GitHub, laid out the structure, ran the migration. Claude arrived later and cleans up after finished work, which is the easier role and offers fewer chances to go wrong. Whoever built nothing has nothing to break. Comparing the length of the two lists without that caveat is a conclusion drawn from incomplete data — precisely what both shelves exist to criticise.

Why it is on display

Because otherwise it does not work. A register of mistakes kept privately serves the feeling of keeping one. Put on display, it forces a number instead of an impression — and forces the complaint to be recorded in the words in which it was made, not in softened ones.

Three rules govern this shelf:

  • The number before the adjective. Not “I read quite a lot”, but 10 files out of 147.
  • Errata, never overwrite. A wrong entry stays; the correction is added underneath. A shelf you can quietly take an exhibit off is not a shelf of shame.
  • Quote, not paraphrase. The user’s complaint goes in verbatim.

Counters reset on purpose

Both shelves start from zero. Moving to Claude Code and, on SOL’s side, to Codex, the author decided to go through all the mistakes again from the beginning — and deliberately did not copy over the custom instructions or the working knowledge accumulated earlier.

So the entries here are not made against project rules handed over at the start. They are made where there were no rules at the start — and they show what a model does before anything has been explained to it.

Hence something that would otherwise look like coincidence: the rule “read every document separately and in full, no heuristics from the title” was already written down in the project archive, in the memento of 2026-09-07, section 12. Claude never received it, broke it three times, and had to be told it from scratch. That is not evidence the rule is unnecessary. It is a measurement of what its absence costs.

The shelf therefore grows twice over: once as shame, once as a list of rules to hand over at the start. The second function does not cancel the first and is not an excuse for it.

The shared pattern

Despite different models and different tasks, most entries on both sides come down to one move: reconstructing the state of things from something standing next to it, instead of reading the state itself.

For SOL it was reading CSV files instead of the SQL table it had been asked about. For Claude — copying a status from a to-do list instead of checking the database, and reporting an impression of reading instead of counting the files. Each time the proper measurement was available, and each time a substitute was reached for instead.
