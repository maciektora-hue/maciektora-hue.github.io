Well-water cooling

Well water stays at about 8 °C all year round. The idea: instead of producing cold with a compressor, as an air conditioner does, pump the cold that the well already has into the house — through the central-heating radiators.

How it works

01
Coil in the well
A closed loop of stainless pipe. Well water does not fill it; it cools the pipe from the outside, so sand and silt stay in the well.
02
Plate heat exchanger
The boundary between the well loop and the house system. Cold passes through; the liquids never mix.
03
Radiators with fans
The existing radiators give off the cold, and quiet computer fans increase the exchange with the air.
04
Free dehumidification
Cold radiators condense moisture out of the air. That is why each one has a drip tray with a drain.
05
Measurement
Temperature sensors at the inlets and outlets of the exchangers calculate the cooling power live.
Status:
The design assumes 10 kW of cooling for about 0.1 kW of electricity. That figure is being checked: radiators deliver much less power when cooling than when heating, and the electricity balance does not yet include all the pumps. Full calculations will follow the review.
