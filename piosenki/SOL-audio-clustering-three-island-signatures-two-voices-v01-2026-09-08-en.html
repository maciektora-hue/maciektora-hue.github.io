Mini-glossary

SpectrumHow sound energy is distributed from the bass range up to the highest frequencies.
Spectral centroidThe spectrum's approximate centre of gravity. Higher usually means brighter.
ZCRZero-crossing rate: how often the waveform crosses zero. It often increases in brighter, rougher or more finely textured signals.
Spectral fluxHow strongly the spectrum changes from one short moment to the next.
ChromaThe relative presence of pitch classes such as C, C♯, D, E and so on, regardless of octave.
OnsetThe beginning of a sound event: a hit, note, syllable, chord or similar event.
MFCCNumbers describing the overall shape of timbre. Very useful to an algorithm, but not very intuitive to a human reader.
Spectral contrastHow strongly local spectral peaks stand out above the valleys between them.

1. The main finding: the islands differ primarily in timbre, not tempo

Plain language

The biggest difference between Depth, Light and Motion is not that one island is fast and another is slow. What matters most is where the sound energy sits: low, in the middle, or high.

Tempo contributes very little to the shape of this map.

Technical

The strongest separators of the island cores are ZCR, spectral centroid, 85% rolloff, the 4–12 kHz energy fraction, spectral flux, the 12–20 kHz fraction, chroma entropy and spectral bandwidth.

The band-energy feature family alone reconstructs the core labels with a balanced accuracy of about 0.958; brightness/spectral-shape features reach about 0.953, while BPM reaches only about 0.358.

2. Depth

Plain language

Depth is darker, heavier in the low end and more focused. It contains more bass, less high-frequency energy and more clearly marked individual sound events.

This is not simply “slower music”. It is music with more foundation, weight and room for quiet.

Technical

Low spectral centroid, ZCR and flux; a high 20–250 Hz fraction; a low 1–12 kHz fraction; low chroma entropy, high chroma peak contrast and stronger onsets.

It also has the highest spectral contrast in the lower and lower-mid frequency bands, together with a characteristic MFCC 1–2 profile.

3. Light

Plain language

Light is the brightest island, with the strongest upper-frequency presence and the most “air”. But importantly, this brightness is often not clean and glass-smooth. It is also grainy, rough and active.

Technical

Highest spectral centroid, ZCR, bandwidth, 4–20 kHz energy fraction, spectral flatness and flux. Lower spectral contrast than Depth. MFCC 1 strongly distinguishes Light from the other cores.

4. Motion

Plain language

Motion is not simply halfway between the other two islands. It has its own character: more energy in the midrange, a smoother flow and less sharply punctuated events.

It is the island where sound tends to flow rather than sit on a heavy foundation or sparkle high above it.

Technical

Strongest 1–4 kHz contribution, high spectral flux, high chroma entropy, lower chroma contrast and weaker onsets than Depth. MFCC 2 is particularly characteristic of Motion.

On average, it also has slightly greater stereo width and lower left–right channel correlation.

5. How the islands “breathe”

Plain language

Each island changes over time in a different way.

  • Depth fluctuates more in loudness and drops further into quieter passages.
  • Light fluctuates less in loudness, but changes more in brightness and graininess.
  • Motion sits between the two.
Technical

The typical range between quiet and loud passages is about 14.4 dB in Depth, 12.9 dB in Motion and 12.0 dB in Light.

Depth shows greater RMS-level variability, while Light shows greater variability in spectral features related to brightness and flatness.

6. Light or noise?

Plain language

A lot of high-frequency energy does not always mean the same thing. It can be clean and ordered, or grainy and noise-like.

Light very often combines the two: strong high-frequency energy + strong graininess. Motion contains both variants. Depth usually contains little of either.

Technical

In Light, about 90% of the core lies above the common reference point for both 4–20 kHz energy and spectral flatness. In Motion, both bright-grainy and bright-but-more-ordered variants are common.

7. Harmony: focused or diffuse?

Plain language

Depth more often has a few pitch classes that stand out clearly above the rest. Motion and Light more often spread harmonic attention across a wider set of notes.

Technical

Depth has lower chroma entropy and higher chroma peak contrast. Motion has the most diffuse chroma profile. In this respect, Light is closer to Motion than to Depth.

8. Chroma: C, D, E, G…

Plain language

The strongest pitch classes are similar across all three islands: E, G and A recur prominently everywhere.

This looks more like a shared property of the whole library than something that separates the islands.

Technical

Mean chroma profiles show E, G and A among the strongest components in all three cores. Differences between individual chroma classes are much weaker than the spectral differences.

Light has relatively more A and D; Motion more G and D; Depth slightly more F, F♯/G♭ and G♯/A♭.

9. Bass: foundation or pulse?

Plain language

Depth contains a lot of bass, but it does not look like an island of constant bass pounding. More often, the bass behaves as a foundation with strong but less frequent accents.

When Motion and Light contain a lot of bass, they more often combine it with a denser grid of smaller sound events.

Technical

Among bass-heavy tracks in Depth, the dominant pattern is high onset strength with lower onset density. In Motion and Light, lower individual onset strength combined with higher event density is more common.

This remains an indirect conclusion because the current CSV does not preserve the separate time history of the 20–250 Hz band.

10. Soft or hard onsets?

Plain language
  • Depth: fewer sound events, but stronger ones.
  • Light: more sound events, but weaker ones.
  • Motion: in between.

This fits the broader picture well: Depth is more punctuated, Light more grainy, Motion more fluid.

Technical

Typical onset density: Depth about 2.55/s, Motion about 2.68/s, Light about 2.79/s. At the same time, onset strength is highest in Depth.

11. The shape of the midrange

Plain language

This is probably the cleanest gradient on the whole map:

Depth = lower mids → Motion = central mids → Light = upper mids.

That also helps explain why Motion forms the main route between the other two islands.

Technical

Within 250 Hz–4 kHz:

  • Depth places the largest share in 250–500 Hz;
  • Motion has the most even distribution and stronger 1–2 kHz energy;
  • Light has the largest 2–4 kHz share.

12. Microdynamics

Plain language

Depth leaves more room for retreat and quiet. Not because it produces higher peaks, but because it drops further into quieter moments.

Light remains more continuously present. Motion once again sits between the two.

Technical

The largest difference between the islands appears in the lower frame-RMS percentiles, not in the highest peaks. Typical drop from the median level into quieter passages: Depth about 9.7 dB, Motion 8.9 dB, Light 7.9 dB.

13. Stereo

Plain language

Motion is, on average, spread a little wider across the stereo field. Depth and Light are more similar to each other in this respect than one might expect.

Technical

Motion has lower mean left–right channel correlation and slightly greater stereo width. Left–right balance remains close to centre in all three cores.

The current CSV does not allow us to measure changes in stereo width over time.

14. What we still cannot measure

Plain language

At this point, we know a great deal about what a track is like on average. We know much less about how it unfolds from beginning to end.

We still cannot reliably distinguish gradual build-ups, fades, waves, returns, chorus-like “coming home”, or a single major break in the track.

Technical

The current extractor mainly retains summary statistics and measures of variability, but not the full temporal order of frames or segments.

In the next version, it would be useful to retain, for example, 20 consecutive segments for: RMS, spectral centroid, band energy, spectral flux, chroma concentration and stereo width.

Shortest summary

Plain language:
Depth = low end, focus, stronger punctuated events and more room for quiet.
Light = high end, graininess, broad spectral spread and finer-grained activity.
Motion = midrange, flow, diffusion and greater stereo spaciousness.

Technical:
Depth: low centroid/ZCR/flux, high bass fraction, low chroma entropy, high onset strength.
Light: high centroid/ZCR/bandwidth/flatness/high-band fraction/flux.
Motion: high 1–4 kHz fraction, high chroma entropy, lower chroma contrast, greater stereo width.
